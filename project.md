Yes — I understand the positioning. This should **not feel like an online coffee shop**. It should feel like a **professional Kenyan coffee & tea export company** whose website gives international buyers confidence that:

* They are based in Kenya.
* They are a **licensed coffee exporter**.
* They source/grow and nurture coffee in Kenya.
* They handle **large-volume exports**.
* They supply international markets across **Africa, Europe, UK, USA, Asia, Japan, etc.**
* They deal in specific **Kenyan coffee grades/types** and teas.
* A foreign buyer can quickly understand **what they export, where it comes from, and how to contact them for a bulk order**.

The current content you gave is a good starting point, but I'd structure the website much more like a **B2B export company** than a consumer coffee website.

## 16. One thing I'd change from the current website

From the information you pasted, things like:

> My Waitlist
> Shop Coffee
> FAQ

are consumer/e-commerce language.

For this new website, I'd probably replace **Shop Coffee** with:

**Our Products**

and replace **My Waitlist** with:

**Request a Quote**

Because the commercial objective is not:

> "Buy one bag."

It's:

> **"We want international companies to contact Rovil and buy coffee/tea in bulk."**

That's the core strategy I'd build the whole website around.

And your **Kenya → world 3D export visualization** can become the signature visual that makes the site memorable.

If we build the content around that strategy, the next step I'd recommend is to map out the **exact homepage section-by-section (Hero → credibility → coffee → tea → farm → export map → buyer CTA → footer)** and then write the actual copy for each section before touching the code.
